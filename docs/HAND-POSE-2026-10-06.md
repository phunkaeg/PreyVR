# VR hand pose: the hands where the player's hands are

Branch `jordi/polishing` (formerly `jordi/hand-pose`; from `collab/integration` @ `368118b`). Measured in Prey with a
headless OpenXR mock (1280 × 1440 per eye, Quest 3 field of view), **not yet in a headset**.
Every change is switchable at runtime for A/B; `pose.mode 0` plus `near.fov 1257` is the
original behaviour. The arms above the wrist (shoulder, elbow) are in
[ARM-POSE-2026-10-06.md](ARM-POSE-2026-10-06.md).

## What was wrong (measured, mock, same controller poses)

| Weapon | Original: palm to controller (right / left) | New |
|---|---|---|
| GLOO | 12.0 / 6.2 cm | 0.0 / 0.0 cm |
| Huntress Boltcaster | 13.0 / 5.4 cm, right palm normal 166° off | 0.0 / 0.0 cm, 13.5° |
| Pistol | 13.7 / 6.7 cm | 0.0 / 0.0 cm |
| Wrench | 9.3 / 14.9 cm | 0.0 / 0.0 cm |

"Palm to controller" is the distance from the game palm's grip point (bones after the native
ADIK pass) to the OpenXR grip origin. On top of that, the hands were **drawn** at 0.66 of
their true angle from the eye (see 1).

1. **The near pass was drawn with a FOV the world did not use.** `VrMode` set
   `r_DrawNearFoV` to `2·atan(tan 60° · h/w)`, which equals the world's vertical FOV only when
   Prey's FOV slider is 120°. This machine has `cl_hfov 98`: the world rendered (and was
   declared to OpenXR) at a vertical tangent of 1.294, while arms and weapon rendered at 1.949.
   Since the IK places the hands in world space, they appeared at 1.294/1.949 = 0.66 of their
   angle, pulled towards the centre: short arms, a weapon above the real hand. **Fix:** while
   VR is active the near FOV follows the vertical FOV the world camera actually rendered
   (`RenderedEyeTangents`). On a 120° slider it computes the same value as before.
   `near.follow 0|1`; an explicit `near.fov N` turns following off.
2. **The IK target is the wrist, the grip pose's origin is the middle of the fist.** Placing the
   wrist at the controller put the palm about 6 cm ahead of the real one; the 65% linear reach
   compression then pulled it back by an amount that depended on the distance to the animated
   shoulder. **Fix:** the wrist goal is the controller minus the palm offset (in the hand's own
   frame), for both hands.
3. **The free hand's orientation came from an instant of the animation.** The left-hand
   calibration stores `controller⁻¹ · animatedWrist` at re-bind, so its angle to the controller
   was whatever the weapon's animation was doing then (GLOO: palm up; wrench: a fist).
   **Fix:** the game hand's anatomical frame (wrist→knuckles, thumb side, palm normal, measured
   from its bones) is mapped onto the player's real hand frame, which the OpenXR grip pose
   defines by spec (+X palm normal, out of the palm on the left hand and into it on the right;
   −Z through the fist from pinky to thumb). Weapon independent, nothing captured.
4. **The free hand's fingers were the weapon animation's.** **Fix:** one relaxed open hand
   (fixed per-joint flexion about each phalanx's local Z, the rig's own metacarpal arch).
   `pose.curl <percent>`.
5. **Two-handed support kept the controller's rotation.** **Fix:** while the left grip holds a
   two-handed weapon, the left hand takes the **native** two-handed hold: the animation's own
   left wrist (and fingers), carried rigidly with the written right wrist. Blended with the
   free hand by the existing support blend. The support region is now computed from the native
   left **palm** (the controller stands for the palm). Measured: Boltcaster and GLOO acquire,
   hold, steer and release.
6. **The muzzle helper's roll held the Boltcaster upside down.** Its authored helper frame put
   the right palm facing out (166° from the real palm) although Prey's own idle holds it palm-in
   (measured with `ik.drive 0`). **Fix:** the barrel stays on the aim ray and the weapon is
   rolled about it by the least-squares angle that lays the drawn hand on the real hand
   (`pose.roll 0|1`). Measured corrections: Boltcaster 174.4°, GLOO 0.1°, pistol 0.6°,
   wrench −2.3°. The Disruptor's hard-coded `fx_muzzle` exception is the same class of issue.
7. **Reach.** 65% linear compression moved every hand off its controller even at rest.
   **Fix (`pose.reach 1 85`):** exact up to 85% of the arm's reach, then a smooth approach to
   it (slope 1 at the knee, never the straight-arm singularity). The arm is short (0.516 m
   shoulder→wrist), so the native two-bone solve is allowed to lengthen it to 120%
   (`pose.stretch`, native limit 1.25): with the controller 65 cm ahead of the eyes the hand is
   1.5 cm short (7.7 cm at 100%); the GLOO foregrip becomes reachable. Side views show no
   visible rubber arm at 120%.

## Instruments

- `pose.report`: palm error, hand-axis errors and both hand frames in each controller's grip
  frame, the weapon roll, and the runtime's **grip pose relative to its aim pose**. Logged to
  `PreyVR.log` every 30 s (`pose_report`), so a headset session records the real Touch
  grip/aim relation with no command typed.
- `pose.marks`: both hands as drawn (bones after the native pass), as segments in the app's
  OpenXR space. The mock compositor draws them over each eye with the controllers' axes; the
  rendered glove must lie on them. This is what exposed the near-FOV mismatch.
- `ik.skel`: one-shot dump of the owning rig (names, parents, relative and absolute poses before
  and after ADIK). The joint table's parent is the low 16 bits at `+0x18` (verified against
  the pose).

## Verification

- `tests/HandPoseTests.cpp` (CTest `hand_pose`): names and hierarchy, grip-frame definition,
  the measured hand frame and its mirror, frame mapping, relaxed pose, grip point, soft reach
  (1:1 inside the knee, monotonic, continuous, slope 1 at the knee), roll recovery.
- Mock captures: the rendered hands lie on their measured skeletons in every capture after the
  near-FOV fix; four weapons, three wrist rolls, reach 10–40 cm, two-handed GLOO/Boltcaster.

## Limits

- **Not seen in a headset.** The mock's grip-from-aim transform is an estimate (40° pitch,
  (0, −3, +5) cm); the mod's logged `gripInAim` from a real Touch should replace it.
- The fist tube's lean (15°) and the grip point (55% of wrist→knuckles, 3.5 cm out of the palm)
  are anatomical estimates: `pose.grip <permille> <mm> <mm> <deci-deg>`.
- The weapon hand's pitch relative to the real hand is the weapon's (barrel on the aim ray).
- Psi powers, carrying objects and the Q-Beam were not tested.
